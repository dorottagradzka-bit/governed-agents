## Follow-up: mindfulness result checked in the authors' coding database

I obtained the meta-analysis coding database from the authors' OSF project (review-only link; raw data not reproduced here). Findings:

- In the published version mindfulness is not a separate antecedent. It is coded inside the broader category "beliefs about personal control", so the paper reports no stand-alone mindfulness result.
- Mindfulness appears in 7 samples from 5 research teams; 3 samples come from one team.
- An exploratory random-effects reanalysis (one value per sample, Fisher z) gives correlations between −0.07 and −0.29 across the five technostressors, with very high heterogeneity (I² 77–92%). For general trait mindfulness only (4 samples) the range is −0.12 to −0.23, and three of five confidence intervals include zero.
- The preprint's strongest figure (−0.47 with techno-complexity) does not hold in the final data at that size.
- The strongest correlations come from an IT-mindfulness measure in Langer's tradition (mindful use of technology), not from meditative mindfulness.

Decision: the claim "mindfulness protects against technostress" is not supported. Citable form: people with higher mindfulness report slightly lower technostress in cross-sectional studies, with large differences between studies. Publishing the reanalysis itself requires the authors' consent, because the data link is review-only.
